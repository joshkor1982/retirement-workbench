How to Use

No official list says which agents or lenders are "VA qualified". Any VA-approved lender can make a VA loan, so get Loan Estimates from at least three (a bank, a credit union, and a mortgage company) on the same day and compare them below. For agents, ask whether they hold NAR's Military Relocation Professional (MRP) certification and how many VA buyers they have closed; sellers and listing agents sometimes steer away from VA offers, and an agent who knows the process keeps yours competitive. VA Funding Fee

How This Works

VA charges a one-time funding fee on the loan amount, set by your down payment and whether you have used a VA loan before (first use: 2.15% under 5% down, 1.5% at 5%, 1.25% at 10%; after first use: 3.3%, 1.5%, 1.25%). You pay no fee if you receive VA disability compensation, or would except that you get retired pay instead, or have a rating from a pre-discharge claim before closing. Rated later? You can ask VA for a refund. Rates as published on va.gov.

Questions to Ask an Agent
  • How many VA buyers have you closed in the last two years, and how did you keep those offers competitive?
  • Do you hold the Military Relocation Professional (MRP) certification?
  • Can you work with me remotely until I arrive, including video tours?
  • What is in your buyer agreement: your fee, who pays it, and how long it lasts?
  • Which inspectors and appraisal issues do you see most with VA loans here?

Questions to Ask a Lender
  • How many VA loans did you close last year, and do you service them or sell them?
  • What are your lender fees (Loan Estimate section A), and will you waive any?
  • Can you lock today, for how long, and what does an extension cost?
  • Do you count my retired pay and VA compensation as income, and what documents do you need for each?
  • Will you pull my Certificate of Eligibility (COE) for me?
